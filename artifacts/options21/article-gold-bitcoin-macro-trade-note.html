Gold & Bitcoin: Rates, Risk and Liquidity

A retail-investor guide to the near-term macro forces driving both assets — and why gold and Bitcoin can respond very differently to the same market shock.

👤
Paul Wise
Options21 · AFSL 247412
Published17 Sep 2026
Read time14 min
CategoryMacro & Geopolitics
The key macro signal
Watch the US 10-year Treasury yield. Elevated or rising yields can pressure both assets, particularly Bitcoin. A clear reversal lower in yields would improve the backdrop for gold and Bitcoin, especially if the US dollar also softens.
Key message

Gold and Bitcoin are both sensitive to interest rates, but they are not the same trade. Gold is caught between high bond yields and a stronger US dollar on one side, and geopolitical risk and falling yields on the other. Bitcoin is currently more exposed to global liquidity, risk appetite and leverage, so it can be more vulnerable when rates and bond yields rise.

Executive summary

Gold: Near term, gold faces pressure if US bond yields and the US dollar continue rising. However, geopolitical uncertainty can create safe-haven demand. Gold’s strongest setup would be falling yields and a softer dollar while geopolitical risk remains elevated.

Bitcoin: Near term, Bitcoin is more vulnerable to tighter monetary policy, high bond yields and any forced reduction in leveraged risk positions. It would benefit most from falling yields, a weaker dollar and improving global risk appetite.

The key macro signal: Watch the US 10-year Treasury yield. If it remains around elevated levels or pushes higher, both assets can struggle, particularly Bitcoin. A clear reversal lower in yields would improve the backdrop for both.

1. Why interest rates matter

The simplest way to think about the current environment is: when investors can earn a higher return from cash and government bonds, assets that do not pay interest must compete harder for capital.

Rates ↑ → Bond yields ↑ → US dollar can strengthen → Liquidity tightens → Pressure on gold and Bitcoin

Current backdrop. On 16 September, the US Federal Reserve raised its target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%. Reuters reported that most policymakers projected at least one further increase during 2026. Around the same period, the US 10-year Treasury yield moved through the 5% area, reinforcing pressure on rate-sensitive assets.

2. Gold: two forces pulling in opposite directions

Gold is unusual because the same geopolitical shock can create both a bullish and a bearish effect.

DriverLikely effect on goldWhy
Higher Treasury yieldsNegativeGold pays no interest. Higher bond yields increase the opportunity cost of holding it.
Stronger US dollarNegativeGold is priced globally in US dollars. A stronger dollar can make gold more expensive for non-US buyers.
Geopolitical tensionPositiveInvestors may seek gold as a traditional safe-haven asset during periods of uncertainty.
Higher oil / inflation fearsMixedInflation concerns can support gold, but if they force central banks to keep raising rates, higher yields can overwhelm that support.
Falling yieldsPositiveLower bond yields reduce gold’s opportunity cost and can improve demand.

Near-term interpretation for gold

Base case: a volatile, two-way market. If yields and the US dollar remain high, gold may struggle to make sustained progress even while geopolitical risk remains elevated. If yields turn lower, the same geopolitical backdrop can become much more supportive for gold.

A favourable gold combination: US 10-year yield ↓ + US dollar ↓ + geopolitical uncertainty remains elevated.

3. Bitcoin: more sensitive to liquidity and risk appetite

Bitcoin is often described as “digital gold”, but in shorter market cycles it has frequently behaved more like a high-beta risk asset. When liquidity tightens and investors reduce leverage, Bitcoin can fall quickly. When liquidity improves and risk appetite returns, it can also rebound quickly.

DriverLikely effect on BitcoinWhy
Fed tighteningNegativeHigher policy rates reduce liquidity and raise the return available on lower-risk assets.
High Treasury yieldsNegativeHigher yields increase the required return from risky assets and can reduce speculative demand.
Stronger US dollarUsually negativeA stronger dollar often coincides with tighter global financial conditions.
Risk-off equity marketsNegativeBitcoin can trade with technology and other high-beta assets when investors are cutting risk.
Falling yields / easier liquidityPositiveLower yields and improving liquidity can support renewed demand for high-beta assets.
Yen carry-trade unwindPotentially negativeA stronger yen and higher Japanese rates can force leveraged investors to reduce positions across global risk assets.

Near-term interpretation for Bitcoin

Base case: Bitcoin has a more difficult macro setup while US yields remain elevated and central banks are leaning toward tighter policy. The risk is amplified if Japan raises rates and a stronger yen causes leveraged carry trades to be unwound. Bitcoin’s backdrop would improve materially if global bond yields reverse lower and the US dollar softens.

A favourable Bitcoin combination: US 10-year yield ↓ + US dollar ↓ + equity risk appetite ↑ + no major deleveraging shock.

4. Practical scenario matrix

This is a framework for interpreting the market, not a forecast or trading instruction.

Yields fall, dollar fallsSupportiveSupportiveEasier financial conditions improve the backdrop for both assets.

5. The five-market dashboard to watch

  • US 10-year Treasury yield: The most important macro gauge. Rising yields generally tighten conditions; falling yields improve the backdrop.
  • US Dollar Index (DXY): A rising dollar is usually a headwind for both gold and Bitcoin; a falling dollar can be supportive.
  • USD/JPY: A sharp fall can signal yen strength and potential carry-trade deleveraging.
  • Gold: Watch whether gold can rise even when yields are firm. That can indicate strong safe-haven demand.
  • Bitcoin: Watch whether Bitcoin confirms or diverges from QQQ and other risk assets. Persistent weakness can indicate tighter liquidity or deleveraging.

6. A simple retail-investor decision framework

Before reacting to a move in gold or Bitcoin, ask these three questions:

  1. Are US bond yields rising or falling?
  2. Is the US dollar strengthening or weakening?
  3. Is the market in risk-on mode or risk-off mode?

Then apply the distinction: gold can benefit from fear itself; Bitcoin generally needs the financial conditions around that fear to remain supportive. If fear causes yields to surge and investors to cut leverage, gold may hold up better while Bitcoin may come under greater pressure.

7. Key risks to this view

  • Gold and Bitcoin can move sharply for asset-specific reasons even when the macro backdrop is unchanged.
  • Central-bank guidance can change quickly as inflation, employment and energy prices change.
  • Geopolitical events can create overnight gaps and rapid reversals.
  • Bitcoin trades 24/7 and can experience much larger percentage moves than gold or broad equity indices.
  • A macro framework is useful for context, but it does not identify precise entry, exit or position-sizing levels.
Bottom line
  • Gold: Better protected if geopolitical stress remains high, but still vulnerable to high yields and a strong dollar. A fall in Treasury yields would materially improve the setup.
  • Bitcoin: More vulnerable while rates, yields and the dollar remain elevated. The clearest improvement would be a reversal lower in yields, a softer dollar and a return of risk appetite.
  • Gold and Bitcoin should not be treated as interchangeable expressions of the same macro view. Gold can benefit from fear; Bitcoin generally needs supportive liquidity around that fear.
