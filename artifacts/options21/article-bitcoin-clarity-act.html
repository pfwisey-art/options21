Why Bitcoin Has Fallen

A demand, liquidity and confidence reversal from the October 2025 peak — and what the CLARITY Act, ETF outflows, and a 50% drawdown really mean for investors.

Key Takeaway

Bitcoin fell because exceptional buying turned into persistent selling. A clearer US rulebook may help demand return over time, but only returning capital — ETF flows, trading volume and broader liquidity — can reverse a demand-driven decline.

1. Why Bitcoin Has Fallen Since October 2025

Bitcoin's broad downtrend began after an exceptionally crowded peak. The path down has included sharp rebounds, but the sequence below shows how a single leverage event became a prolonged shortage of demand.

DatePrice / EventFundamental Significance
6 Oct 2025>US$126,000Record demand, softer US dollar and regulatory optimism produced the cycle peak.
10–11 OctUS$104,783 lowUS-China tariff shock met thin liquidity. More than US$19 billion of leveraged crypto positions liquidated.
17 Nov 2025Below US$90,000ETF flows reversed and rate-cut expectations weakened. US$3.7 billion left US spot Bitcoin ETFs after 10 October.
31 Jan 2026~US$78,720Firmer dollar and fear of tighter system liquidity reinforced the decline.
Q2 2026–US$4.9bn ETF flowTraditional spot demand stayed negative while leverage drove price moves, not fresh cash buying.
6 Aug 2026~US$64,500Market is stabilising, but institutional demand and trading volume have not returned to prior-cycle strength.

Six forces drove the decline, in roughly the order they mattered:

01
The peak was built on exceptional marginal demand
Record fund inflows, friendlier US policy expectations and a weak dollar pulled future demand forward. Once that buying slowed, the market had little valuation support because Bitcoin produces no earnings or yield.
02
October broke the market's leverage structure
A US-China tariff announcement was the trigger, but the scale of the fall came from leverage and thin liquidity. More than US$19 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated, converting an external shock into self-reinforcing selling.
03
The ETF rails changed direction
The same spot ETFs that created a persistent bid in the bull market became a source of redemptions. Q2 2026 alone recorded US$4.9 billion of net US outflows. Glassnode estimates June funds returned about 65,800 BTC to the market — their worst month on record.
04
The macro backdrop stayed restrictive
The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July, with June CPI at 3.5% year-on-year and core PCE at 3.3%. Higher real yields compete directly with a non-yielding asset such as Bitcoin.
05
The expected regulatory catalyst was delayed
Investors had priced a friendlier US regime. Delays over the CLARITY Act removed a near-term catalyst and softened demand expectations — but did not damage the network itself.
06
Corporate treasury demand stopped looking one-way
Strategy's verified sale of 1,638 BTC (27 July – 2 August) was small against its 842,138 BTC holdings but symbolically important: even the largest corporate holder may sell to meet financing needs.
What Did Not Cause the Fall

There is no evidence that Bitcoin's supply cap, issuance schedule or core settlement network failed. The drawdown is best understood as a repricing of demand, liquidity and risk appetite around an unchanged protocol.

2. The CLARITY Act in Context

Because a friendlier US regime had been priced in, the progress of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act matters to sentiment — but as one input to demand, not as the reason the market fell. The bill would replace overlapping and uncertain treatment with a federal framework for issuing, trading, holding and supervising digital assets, spanning the SEC-CFTC boundary, registered market infrastructure, AML and sanctions, bank custody, customer protection, stablecoin yield and tokenisation.

DateMilestoneWhat It Means
17 Jul 2025House passageH.R. 3633 passed 294–134, establishing a strong bipartisan starting point.
14 May 2026Senate committeeSenate Banking advanced its version 15–9 and sent it toward the floor.
22 Jul 2026Merged textA 616-page Banking-Agriculture work product updated AML, DeFi, stablecoin-yield, customer-property and ethics provisions.
6 Aug 2026Current positionNo full Senate passage yet. The text remains exposed to negotiation, floor timing and the midterm calendar.

Bitcoin is already the least legally ambiguous major crypto asset in the United States, so its largest benefit from CLARITY is indirect: clearer spot-market supervision, more usable bank and custody rails and lower compliance uncertainty for institutions.

Regulatory Context

CLARITY can improve the legal and institutional plumbing around digital assets. It cannot, by itself, replace the missing spot demand — which is why it belongs among the contributing factors to Bitcoin's fall, not at the centre of it.

3. Is the Market Building a Bottom?

The evidence is mixed. Valuation and seller-exhaustion measures are moving into regions associated with prior bottoms, but the demand side remains incomplete. That argues for separating "less downside pressure" from "a confirmed new uptrend."

IndicatorConstructive EvidenceWhat Is Still Missing
Seller exhaustionProfitability compression and long-term-holder capitulation resemble late-bear conditions.Glassnode says the exhaustion gauge is near, but not yet at, the floor of prior cycles.
LeverageDerivatives have de-risked substantially from the October shock.Spot-led demand must replace leverage-led rebounds.
ETF demandOutflows have periodically eased and new channels can still attract capital.Sustained net inflows for several weeks, not one or two positive sessions.
Market volumeLower volatility may indicate forced selling is fading.July daily volume remained about 80% below the October peak.
RegulationThe merged CLARITY text is further advanced and more comprehensive than the earlier draft.Full Senate passage, reconciliation and an implementation path.

A durable change in trend would need several of the following to align:

Sustained ETF inflows — the clearest evidence that the traditional marginal buyer has returned.
A more supportive liquidity cycle — cooling inflation, lower energy pressure and credible Fed easing would reduce the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset.
Recovering spot volume and stablecoin liquidity — a durable recovery should be funded by fresh cash and on-chain liquidity, not primarily derivatives leverage.
Corporate treasury net buying — treasury demand needs to exceed sales and financing-related distributions.
Progress on CLARITY — passage would reduce regulatory uncertainty, though the quality of the final text and implementation timetable matter as much as the headline.
Constructive
ETF flows positive; inflation cools; spot volume expands; CLARITY advances

A broader, institutionally funded recovery becomes more credible. Price support has a real foundation.

Base / Unresolved
Legislative progress but no final law; ETF flows oscillate; Fed on hold

Volatile range trading with sharp headline-driven rallies and reversals. No clear trend direction.

Adverse
Bill slips beyond midterms; outflows persist; real rates stay high

Downside remains open and lower price levels may be retested. Corporate sales add to selling pressure.

4. Retail Investor Risk Framework

Whatever happens in Washington, the CLARITY Act would not eliminate Bitcoin's core investment risks. Australian investors should also distinguish direct crypto ownership from regulated products that provide crypto exposure — the legal rights, custody arrangements, fees and disclosure protections can differ materially.

Extreme Volatility
A near-50% drawdown has occurred even during a period of improving institutional access. Larger historical drawdowns have happened before.
No Cash-Flow Anchor
Bitcoin pays no dividend or interest. Price depends entirely on future demand and available liquidity.
Custody & Counterparty Risk
Private keys can be lost or stolen; exchanges and custodians can fail; legal recovery may be limited.
Product & Leverage Risk
ETFs, futures, CFDs, options and treasury-company shares are not equivalent to unleveraged spot Bitcoin. Leverage can produce rapid or total loss.
Regulatory & Tax Risk
Rules can change across jurisdictions. Disposals or crypto-to-crypto transactions may have Australian tax consequences.
Information Risk
Crypto markets trade continuously and react to rumours. Verify legislative status and market data before acting.
Practical Discipline

Know the product, avoid using leverage you cannot absorb, size exposure for very large drawdowns, understand who holds the asset, and read the PDS and TMD where a regulated financial product is involved.

Conclusion
  • Bitcoin fell because the market moved from exceptional marginal buying to persistent marginal selling. The October tariff shock detonated leverage; ETF redemptions removed the institutional bid; inflation and higher real rates reduced liquidity; a delayed regulatory timetable weakened an expected catalyst.
  • The CLARITY Act sits inside that picture as one contributing factor. If enacted, it would give US digital-asset markets a clearer supervisory framework, stronger institutional rails and more defined customer protections — a meaningful long-term positive for Bitcoin, but an indirect one.
  • What matters next is the evidence: sustained ETF inflows, recovering spot volume, lower real-rate pressure, renewed corporate demand and progress on CLARITY.
  • Until those signals align, the market may be building a bottom without yet confirming a durable new uptrend. A favourable law can reduce uncertainty; only returning capital can reverse a demand-driven bear market.
